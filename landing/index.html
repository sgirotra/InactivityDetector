How it works

Set up in an afternoon. Then it just runs.

You keep your suppliers, your reps and your final say. Larder does the reading, the math and the chasing.

1

Show it what you buy

Snap photos of last month's invoices or forward your supplier emails. Larder builds your order guide: items, pack sizes, prices and who sells what.

Reads: "CS 6/#10 TOM CRUSHED" → Crushed tomatoes, 6 × #10 cans
2

Set your pars once

Tell it how much you want on the shelf. Do a quick count on your phone, and it learns your usage from what you sell and what you order.

3

Approve, and it's sent

Before each cutoff you get a drafted order split by supplier, with price changes flagged. Tap approve and it goes out by email, text or portal.

Questions

The things owners ask first.

Does it place orders without asking me?

No. By default every order waits for your approval. If you want, you can let it auto-send routine orders under a dollar limit you set, and it will still tell you what went out.

Do I have to change suppliers?

No. Larder works with the suppliers you already buy from, by email, text or their ordering portal. It will point out when another of your suppliers is cheaper for an item, and you decide whether to move it.

I'm not a tech person. Is this hard to set up?

If you can take a photo of an invoice, you can set it up. During the pilot we do the first setup with you, usually in one sitting.

What happens to my pricing data?

Your invoices and prices are yours. They're used to run your account and are never shared with suppliers or other businesses.

What does it cost?

The pilot is free. After that, pricing is a flat monthly fee sized for small businesses, with no percentage taken from your orders.
